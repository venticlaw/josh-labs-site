Low end

Your 808 is loud, but it still feels small.

An 808 can be loud and still not feel big. That sounds wrong until you have lived it.

You turn it up. The meters move. The room shakes a little. Then you play it somewhere else and the whole bottom of the song disappears.

That is not just a volume problem. It is a low-end translation problem.

Loud is not the same as useful

Low end has to carry weight, work with the kick, survive smaller speakers, and leave space for the vocal and the rest of the track.

When that balance is off, the 808 can trick you. In the studio it feels huge. On a phone it feels gone. In a car it can make the whole mix lean too far toward the bottom.

The Fix Your Sound question

Is the bass helping the record translate?

That is the difference between a producer decision and a meter decision.

Fix Your Sound should help producers catch likely low-end masking, sub energy that does not translate, and headroom disappearing before the mix is ready.

Why this belongs in the campaign

The 808 problem is a clean entry point because producers already understand the pain. Nobody needs a lecture to know the feeling of a beat that only bangs in one place.
