Mix language

Mud, harshness, and headroom are not fancy words.

People use them like secret engineer code, but producers feel them every day.

Mud is when the track feels crowded in the middle and low-mid area. Harshness is when the exciting part starts feeling painful. Headroom is the space you leave before the mix starts running into the ceiling.

That is not a college lecture. That is what happens when the beat is almost there, but something keeps making you second-guess the bounce.

The problem is naming the problem

The hard part is not always hearing the issue. The hard part is knowing what kind of issue it is.

If the vocal feels bad, a producer might turn it down, brighten it, compress it harder, add another effect, blame the beat, or blame the recording. Sometimes one of those works. Sometimes it makes the mix worse.

Name the problem before reaching for the fix.

Loudness does not fix bad balance

EBU's loudness guidance emphasizes loudness metering and normalization instead of relying only on peak levels. Spotify also gives creators Spotify-specific loudness and true-peak recommendations for reducing unwanted distortion when tracks are normalized.

If the mix is already crowded, painful, or clipped, loudness will not save it. Loud just makes the problem more confident.
