Mix language

Mud, harshness, and low end: a producer's translation map.

The best mix language is not fancy. It helps you stop guessing.

Producers already feel mix problems before they have the words for them. The vocal makes your ears tired. The beat feels cloudy. The bass is huge at home and missing in the car. The master is loud but somehow smaller.

Good tools should turn those feelings into decisions.

Mud

Mud usually feels like too much stuff living in the same low-mid neighborhood. The mix gets thick, the vocal loses space, and turning things up makes everything worse.

Harshness

Harshness is the part of "bright" that stops being exciting and starts being annoying. It can come from vocals, synths, cymbals, distortion, or stacked sounds fighting for attention.

Low end

Low end is not one problem. It can be missing weight, too much sub, kick and bass fighting, stereo low-end confusion, or harmonics that do not speak on smaller systems.

Headroom and loudness

Headroom is the space your mix leaves before the ceiling. Loudness is how loud playback feels. Peak level is not the whole story, which is why ITU-R BS.1770 and EBU R 128 matter as measurement references.

Fix the name of the problem first. The move gets easier after that.

That is the common thread between Your Mix Sucks and BassPhat. One names the mix problem. The other gives low end a more focused way to hit.
